# Casino — Vertical Playbook

How to deploy Playbook on a land-based gaming floor: tables, slots, cage, host desk, and the venue around them.

> **Operator note**: The floor is the one vertical where players and staff share a room. That is your advantage. Print and signage do the always-on work, but a dealer who can explain the house edge in one plain sentence beats any poster. Deploy the content and the conversation together.

## The 30-second version

A casino deployment runs on three layers. **Ambient**: signage and display loops that make game literacy part of the room, not a corner poster. **At the point of play**: table tents and quick-reference cards where the decision happens. **In person**: floor staff, cage staff, and hosts who can hold a confident, stigma-free conversation about how the games work. Players read the room long before they read a brochure, so the room itself should say: this house tells you how the math works.

## The player journey

| Moment | Where | What runs there |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| Arrival | Entrance, lobby screens | Brand-level literacy spots in the display loop ([digital display specs](../collateral/environmental/digital-display-specs.md)) |
| Choosing a game | Floor signage, kiosk | [Odds at a glance](../how-to-play/odds-at-a-glance.md) content: house edge by game, plain numbers |
| At the table | Table rail | [Table tents](../collateral/print/table-tent.md) with per-game facts; dealers fluent in the one-sentence versions |
| At the machine | Slot chair, service window | Slots myths content ([myth-busting](../messaging/myth-busting.md), Myths 1 to 6) on screens and cards |
| Player's club desk | Enrollment conversation | [Quick-reference cards](../how-to-play/quick-reference/) as a welcome takeaway; host talking points |
| The pause | Restrooms, ATMs, corridors | [Helpline cards](../collateral/print/helpline-card.md) and [rack cards](../collateral/print/rack-card.md) where a player has a quiet moment |
| Cash-out | Cage | Staff who treat tool questions (limits, breaks) as service, not alarm ([conversation scripts](../collateral/customer-service/conversation-scripts.md)) |

The placement rule: literacy content lives where decisions happen, support content lives where pauses happen. Keep the two tiers apart. A helpline card at the table reads as a warning; the same card at the ATM reads as a service.

## Staff readiness

Content fails on a floor where staff can't back it up. Before launch:

1. Dealers and slot attendants read the [staff FAQ](../collateral/customer-service/staff-faq.md) and the quick refs for their pit or bank.
2. Hosts, cage, and player's club staff work through the [conversation scripts](../collateral/customer-service/conversation-scripts.md), including the Tier 2 handoff.
3. Everyone learns the [stigma-free language](../messaging/stigma-free-language.md) list. The words are the training.

## Jurisdiction overlay

Signage mandates, helpline wording, and venue disclosure rules differ by market. Open your [jurisdiction module](../jurisdictions/) next to this playbook and let it override: mandated text wins, then placement, then style. Modules tagged `land-based` carry the venue-specific detail.

## Measure it

Score the deployment with the [content scorecard](../docs/content-scorecard.md): reach (who saw it), engagement (who used it), and belief shift (what changed). The floor advantage is direct observation: staff hear the myths retreat before any survey shows it.

## Start this week

1. Configure [_brand.yml](../_brand.yml) and render the print set for your top games.
2. Put [odds at a glance](../how-to-play/odds-at-a-glance.md) on the display loop.
3. Brief the frontline with the staff FAQ and language list.
4. Run [Slots Myths Week](../messaging/campaigns.md#campaign-1-slots-myths-week) as the opening campaign.
5. Add table tents pit by pit, and collect what dealers hear back.
